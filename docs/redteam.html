Adversarial benchmark · Phase 8

Detection decay is not uniform

A static detection stack versus an adversary that mutates its own scheme parameters every generation it gets caught. Some controls collapse completely within two generations and never recover. Others never fully evade at all. A single aggregate recall figure could not have shown the difference — and the difference is the whole finding.

Methodology

How the adversary adapts

One genome per typology. Each starts at generation 0's naive default and takes one step toward whichever real-world-plausible direction reduces detectability, every generation it gets caught — freezing once a generation scores zero recall.

The knobs are the injectors' own parameters

Deposit ceiling, cut percentage and hop window, invoice count, hop days, dormancy gap. Nothing invented for the benchmark.

Bounds come from public facts

The cash-reporting line near ₹1,00,000; a mule chain stops being worth running past roughly 35% skimmed per hop. A test asserts no bound equals the rule threshold it is meant to evade.

The adversary never reads the answer key

It knows only the account ids it planted this call, held in local memory, and never reads a rule's tuned constants. The same boundary every detection layer here is held to.

Why one typology is excluded

high_risk_geography is left out on purpose. Its only real evasion move is routing through an unlisted jurisdiction — a categorical choice with no honest continuous knob. Inventing a fake one would have produced a smooth decay curve that meant nothing.

Findings

What collapsed, what held

Collapse is fast and total where it happens

shell_company converges fastest and most completely, and stays at zero afterwards — consistent with the earlier static finding that its rule is unfixable by threshold, now demonstrated under adaptive pressure rather than in a clean-world test. mule_network, the one typology watched by both the rules engine and the graph layer, starts at a perfect 100% and still fully collapses.

Some detection surfaces genuinely resist

structuring and round_tripping never fully evade, even at real parameter extremes — a deposit ceiling of ₹99,000, one rupee under the cash-reporting line, and a hop window stretched to 38 days. Some surfaces are structurally more resistant to this class of evasion than others.

&ldquo;Converged&rdquo; does not mean permanently evaded

dormant_reactivation converges fastest of all and is the least stable convergence — it still averages meaningful recall in the generations after. A frozen genome runs against a freshly regenerated world each generation, so convergence records a first full evasion, not a guarantee. The benchmark reports post-convergence stability as its own number for exactly this reason.

Limitations

What this benchmark does not claim

One seed, one trajectory

The generation-of-convergence numbers are a real measured example, not a population statistic. Averaging over seeds is future work before any of them supports a stronger claim.

Rules and graph only

Whether a trained model decays faster or slower than static thresholds against the same adversary is a real, different and open question.
