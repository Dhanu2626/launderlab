## Research thesis — three unsolved problems we attack

1. **Detection decay** — how fast does a detection stack rot against an adapting adversary? **Answered non-uniformly by Phase 8**: some typologies (shell_company) collapse within 2 generations and stay collapsed; others (structuring, round_tripping) resist full evasion across 8. `python -m launderlab redteam`.
2. **False-positive economics** — cost-per-true-alert measured for every detection config, not guessed (Phases 3–6)
3. **The cross-bank blind spot** — quantify what single banks can't see in multi-bank mule chains, then measure the lift from privacy-preserving co-operation. **Answered by Phase 8.5**: banks flag 75-77% of individual mule accounts and reconstruct 0-6% of the chains they form; hashed-reference co-operation recovers 69-81% of hops without sharing customer data. BIS Project Aurora does this privately — this is the open version. `python -m launderlab multibank`.

**Three tracks:** an analyst doesn't just generate data, they investigate it. Track A —
Simulate (phases 0–2). Track B — Detect (phases 3–6). Track C — Investigate (phase 7).

## Quality bar (non-negotiable)

- `main` never breaks: every slice runs end-to-end before commit
- pytest + ruff green before every commit; GitHub Actions CI on every push
- every phase ships a **visual artifact**, never just tables
- blue-team code must never read `scheme_labels` (ground truth is for scoring only)

| 2026-07-31 | Phase 8.5 | `multibank.py` + `viz.render_multibank()`. The world is partitioned across 4 banks and written to **genuinely separate DuckDB files**, not filtered with a `WHERE` clause: every detector in the project would otherwise have to remember to honour a filter, and one that forgot would silently hand a bank sight of another bank's rows — inventing detection ability that does not exist and inflating the very number this phase exists to measure. With separate files the other bank's rows are simply absent and every existing detector runs unmodified. A test asserts each bank's ledger contains only its own accounts, transactions AND customers (a bank does not hold KYC for another bank's customers). **Two arms, because one alone would have been arithmetic dressed as a finding**: `deliberate` walks the banks in turn so no two consecutive accounts share a bank — a solo bank then reconstructs 0% BY CONSTRUCTION, which is not evidence; `naive` ignores banks entirely, which is what an unsophisticated launderer does, and what a solo bank sees there is genuinely measured. **The result inverted the hypothesis the two-arm design was built to test.** Naive placement still gave a solo bank only 6% (1 reportable stretch across 12 chains), against 0% for deliberate — so spreading a chain across institutions buys the launderer almost nothing, because the blind spot is already near-total by accident. The mechanism is computed rather than asserted (`_solo_reconstructable_runs`): `motifs.find_chains` will not report anything shorter than 2 hops, so a lone intra-bank hop is invisible even to the bank holding both its legs, and with n banks the odds of a reportable same-bank stretch fall as 1/n² per position. **The sharp finding: banks flag 75-77% of the individual mule accounts on their own books while reconstructing 0-6% of the chain.** Local account-level detection works fine; it is the NETWORK that is lost, which is exactly what turns three unrelated alerts into one laundering case. **Co-operation prototype**: each bank publishes, only for accounts it already flagged itself, `HMAC(shared_secret, payment_reference)` + direction + amount + timestamp — never a name, an account id, a balance, or anything at all about an unflagged account. Matching hashed references across banks rebuilds the cross-boundary edges pseudonymously. Recovers **81% of hops (naive) and 69% (deliberate)**. HMAC not a bare hash, deliberately: a plain SHA of a short numeric reference is trivially brute-forced back to the reference, handing every participant a lookup table for payments they were never party to. **A measurement bug found and fixed mid-phase**: scoring the co-operative view on cross-boundary recoveries ALONE penalised the naive arm for intra-bank hops its own bank already held both legs of, and made a chain deliberately spread across banks look *better* covered (69%) than a careless one (50%) — backwards. `cooperative_total_hops` now counts both, and naive correctly comes out ahead at 81%. **Residual disclosure is stated, not glossed**: the coordinator still learns the shape of the inter-bank graph (who transacts with whom, at what volume) even without identities, and a bank that already knows a reference can confirm another bank was in that payment — though it was the counterparty, so it already knew. That is the honest reason this is a prototype rather than a proposal. 14 new tests. |
