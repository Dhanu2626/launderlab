# The pitch — how to explain LaunderLab to anyone

## The 10-second version

Cyber ranges exist for hackers. LaunderLab is one for money laundering: a fake bank,
a robot criminal that keeps learning, and detection software that has to keep up.

## For someone who knows nothing about banking

Criminals "clean" stolen money by passing it through accounts until it looks legal.
Banks must catch this by law — but there's no safe place to practice, because you can't
experiment on real customers. LaunderLab is the flight simulator: a complete fake bank
(thousands of fake people living fake financial lives), a robot criminal hiding dirty
money in the crowd, and detective software hunting it. The robot criminal learns from
getting caught and changes its tricks — so the detective must keep improving, exactly
like the real world.

## Per audience

- **Relative:** "I built a video-game version of a bank where I play both the
  money-launderer and the police — to master the job of catching financial criminals."
- **HR recruiter:** "A crash-test lab for banks' crime-detection systems — like
  crash-testing cars, but for the software that catches money laundering."
- **Technical interviewer:** "An open adversarial simulation range for AML — synthetic
  bank, typology injector, six-model detection stack, and an evolving red team,
  measuring detection decay and cross-bank blind spots — problems the industry
  hasn't solved."
- **LinkedIn:** "Cyber ranges exist for hackers. I'm building one for money laundering."

## What a listener should conclude

1. Didn't wait for a job to learn the job — built the analyst's actual tools first.
2. Understands both sides — has played criminal and investigator hundreds of times.
3. Finishes things — months of public commits, passing tests, and visuals anyone can read.
